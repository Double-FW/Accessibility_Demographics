# Accessibility Demographics

**From worthiness to worthwhile: understanding the customer opportunity of accessibility and inclusive design.**

This is an evolving research and decision resource for marketing, product, design, accessibility, service strategy, research and policy teams. It connects audience evidence to practical opportunities to reach, engage, serve and retain customers across changing needs and circumstances.

It supports people working directly and authorised agents supporting research, analysis and design. It can inform Skills, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and Model Context Protocol (MCP) services; it is not itself a deployed agent or server.

## New to disability demographics?

Start with the [six quick guides](quick-guides/README.md). They explain disability models, functional needs, ageing, customer value, everyday contexts and neurodivergence in plain language, with practical next steps and links to public Double-FW guidance.

## Start with a decision

1. Read [the project objectives](foundations/objectives-and-scope.md) and [definitions](foundations/disability-models-and-access-needs.md).
2. Choose one customer journey and complete the [organisation brief](templates/organisation-brief.md).
3. Estimate the relevant audience using [population methods](methods/estimating-demographics.md), [country evidence](research/uk-eu-us-evidence.md) and [overlap methods](methods/combined-needs-and-reach.md).
4. Follow [service optimisation](guides/service-optimisation.md) and retrieve specialist guidance through the [Double-FW resource map](resources/double-fw-resource-map.md).
5. Assess [commercial and relationship value](methods/customer-and-lifetime-value.md), [brand outcomes](guides/brand-and-engagement.md) and [measurement](guides/research-and-measurement.md).
6. Record findings in the [opportunity assessment](templates/opportunity-assessment.md); validate assumptions with customers.

## Statistics and calculations

Use [Accessibility demographics: statistics and estimation methods](Accessibility_Demographic_Statistics_and_Estimation_Methods.md) for 80 reference entries, including one explicitly excluded estimate, 28 source entries and 17 identified formulae. Entries can contain several measures and overlapping populations: this is not a total audience size or a completed harmonised dataset.

The reference owns the statistical wording and formula definitions. [Structured observations](evidence/population-observations.json) provide a traceable machine-readable view; [the source register](evidence/source-register.md) explains identifier aliases and evidence status. Topic and method guides explain application. If representations disagree, flag and reconcile the discrepancy before calculation.

## Evidence and status

Version 0.3.0 combines the beginner quick guides with the statistical reference, working methods, substantive guides, templates and explicitly synthetic examples. It is not a completed epidemiological review or a validated forecasting model. [The evidence register](evidence/source-register.md) distinguishes checked sources, supplied background and research still required. Country figures describe different populations and must not be ranked as directly comparable measures.

The supplied [UK apparent-disability report](evidence/supplied/Apparent_Disability_Proportions_UK_Report.md) is preserved unchanged. Its proposed 6–8% range and 7% midpoint are **unvalidated hypotheses for this repository**, not accepted audience benchmarks. Read the [methodological review](evidence/apparent-disability-review.md) before reuse. The existing root Word file is retained in the remote repository.

Commercial opportunity is the focus. Successful participation, dignity, choice and autonomy remain outcomes in their own right. A small demographic is not a reason to exclude people, and aggregate commercial gains do not establish an equivalent customer experience.
