## Apply the resources

1. Identify a specific barrier and desired customer outcome in the [opportunity assessment](../templates/opportunity-assessment.md).
2. Retrieve the owning guide, not only its README. Record exact path, version/date and commit or blob identifier where available.
3. Distinguish source guidance from this repository's interpretation. Preserve draft status and evaluation limits.
4. Translate the guidance into a requirement for the relevant foundation, component, content practice or service process.
5. Specify meaningful manual, automatable and intended-user evaluation. Link implementation evidence to the original opportunity.
6. Recheck after source or service changes. Never silently rewrite historical decisions to point at a different revision.

## Example chain

Opportunity: a customer returning to a long financial application should understand what remains to do. Evidence: observed loss of context, with audience scale estimated separately. Guidance: Cognitive UX task continuity. Delivery: persisted progress, clear saved state, current-context summary and explicit handling of changed choices. Validation: real customers resume and understand; measure recovery, effort and continued use. Commercial effect is a hypothesis until assessed.

## Source conflicts and availability

Specialist sources can differ in scope and prescriptiveness. Resolve differences through the actual task, the source's evidence and intended-user evaluation; do not silently merge statements into a universal rule. Record unresolved conflicts and route them for human review.

Do not publish snapshots or links to private resources as public guidance. If a resource becomes unavailable, record the problem, preserve the historical citation and find a reviewed public alternative. Do not invent a replacement. Source metadata is recorded in [the retrieval record](../evidence/double-fw-retrieval.json).
