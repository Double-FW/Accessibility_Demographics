# Worked examples: retail and long-term services

**All numbers and organisations below are synthetic. They are calculation examples, not demographic evidence, forecasts or observed customer outcomes.**

## Example 1: audience membership is not lost business

A service has 100,000 unique adult customers. An illustrative analysis assumes two need groups of 10% and 8%, with compatible joint evidence assumed at 3%. The union is 10% + 8% − 3% = 15%, or 15,000 people. Without joint evidence, only the mathematical bound 10,000–18,000 would be justified.

If annual service revenue is £5 million and the analyst assumes equal average spend, proportional revenue associated with that union is £750,000. This is existing associated revenue under an assumption. It is not revenue lost or recoverable through an improvement.

## Example 2: retail task improvement

A synthetic pilot planning scenario has 20,000 eligible comparison-to-purchase journeys exposed to a specified barrier in one year. A hypothesised improvement in conversion is 2 percentage points. Average order value is £50, contribution margin before project costs is 30%, implementation costs £4,000 and annual maintenance £1,000.

- Additional orders: 20,000 × 0.02 = 400.
- Additional revenue: 400 × £50 = £20,000.
- Additional contribution before project costs: £20,000 × 0.30 = £6,000.
- First-year net contribution: £6,000 − £4,000 − £1,000 = £1,000.
- Break-even absolute conversion improvement: £5,000 / (20,000 × £50 × 0.30) = 0.01667, approximately 1.67 percentage points.

At 0.5 percentage points improvement, net contribution would be −£3,500; at 4 percentage points, £7,000. These are assumption scenarios, not confidence limits. The real study must establish exposure, improvement, margin and costs. Repeat journeys and displaced purchases can affect interpretation.

## Example 3: a long-term relationship

A synthetic pension-service improvement supports customers returning after an absence. An illustrative cohort has 1,000 relationships; over one future year the analyst assumes a 2 percentage point increase in continuation, annual contribution per continuing relationship £100 and discount rate 5%. Assume all other differences are zero.

Incremental expected relationships = 1,000 × 0.02 = 20. Discounted incremental contribution = 20 × £100 / 1.05 = £1,904.76 before implementation and support costs. Pension assets themselves are not revenue. A multi-year model needs explicit survival paths, changing contribution and costs; multiplying this single-year result by a lifespan is not justified.

## Human and brand outcomes

For each example, research understanding, effort, confidence, control and chosen assistance. A better comparison journey may help customers make a better decision without purchasing. A bereavement journey can be worthwhile through reduced burden even when it creates no sale. The opportunity framework must accommodate those outcomes.
