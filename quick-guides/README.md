# Quick guides: accessibility demographics for newcomers

These guides are for people who have not previously considered disability demographics in marketing, product or service decisions. No technical or accessibility background is required. Each takes about three minutes to read, with links for further exploration.

## Use one real customer journey

Pick an activity that matters to your customers: understanding an offer, buying something, changing a pension choice, getting support or returning after an absence. Ask who may encounter difficulty, what the service demands and what would help them participate with confidence.

The aim is useful action: better reach, understanding, choice, successful use and sustained relationships. Evidence can help establish the commercial value without assuming that customers' worth depends on their spending.

## Keep these distinctions in mind

- People with the same diagnosis can need different options.
- A demographic estimate is not proof of a barrier or lost sales.
- Overlapping groups cannot simply be added together.
- Real customer research is needed to understand preferences and outcomes.

The examples are illustrations, not completed customer studies. The EAA guide uses functional criteria as a research lens, not a demographic census. The ageing guide explains evidence patterns without inventing smooth numerical curves where comparable age-band data are missing.
