# Working out the value of customers with access needs

**Separate the business you already have from the additional value an improvement might create.**

Disabled people are already customers. The question is how well the service supports them and what helps the relationship continue. A population percentage can help estimate scale; it cannot identify exactly who is disabled or how much an accessibility change will earn.

## Measured value or estimated value?

| What you have | What you can reasonably report |
| --- | --- |
| Suitable group-level customer and revenue records, used with appropriate permission | Observed revenue for the defined group, explaining who is and is not represented |
| Customer demographics and a relevant population estimate | Modelled audience size and associated revenue under stated assumptions |
| A properly evaluated service improvement | An estimate of additional conversion, retention or cost change, with attribution limits |

Do not infer diagnoses from shopping or browsing behaviour. People who disclose needs may differ from those who do not. A recorded group is not automatically the whole demographic.

## A simple planning calculation

For a relevant customer segment:

**Estimated customers with the need = customer count × applicable prevalence.**

**Estimated associated annual revenue = estimated customers × average annual spend.**

The second calculation assumes the group's average spend matches the chosen comparison. Use actual suitable aggregate figures where available; otherwise show the assumption and a range. Segment by age, geography or product where that improves the match.

**Illustration, with invented inputs:** 10,000 customers × an assumed 10% = 1,000 potentially relevant customers. At an assumed £300 annual spend each, £300,000 is associated existing revenue. It is not £300,000 of lost sales or a promised benefit from an improvement.

## Then investigate the additional opportunity

Identify a specific barrier, how many relevant journeys encounter it, and what changes when the barrier is removed. Additional purchases multiplied by transaction value gives additional revenue. Contribution is the amount left after the relevant variable delivery costs. Apply the appropriate margin and subtract implementation, maintenance and additional costs not already included to estimate the net benefit.

**Illustration:** If an improvement enables 30 additional purchases at £100 each, that is £3,000 revenue. At an assumed 25% contribution margin, it contributes £750 before project costs. These numbers are an example, not an observed result.

## For mortgages, pensions and other long relationships

Estimate how continued usable access affects expected future contribution over a stated period. Include renewal, product changes, changing needs, support and exit. Use the organisation's finance definitions. Mortgage principal, pension assets and deposits are not revenue.

Avoid counting the same person twice when needs overlap, or counting the same retention benefit again within lifetime value. Keep trust, understanding and independent or chosen-assisted use visible even where they cannot be monetised. A small group is not a reason to exclude customers.
