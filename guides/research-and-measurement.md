# Research and measurement

## Research programme

Combine population evidence, lived-experience research, accessibility evaluation and commercial/service analytics. Each answers a different question. An automated scan cannot estimate how many people abandon a service because of a barrier.

1. Frame a journey hypothesis and baseline. State the audience, outcome, unit, period, suspected barrier and proposed change.
2. Recruit inclusively by relevant needs and circumstances, not only diagnosis or an easy-to-reach panel. Include customers who could not complete, prospective customers and chosen support users where appropriate.
3. Use accessible materials, participant-selected modes and fair arrangements for participation. Keep health disclosures optional and separate from ordinary service eligibility.
4. Observe realistic tasks and record comprehension, errors, effort, confidence, control, successful completion and recovery. Treat each participant as an individual.
5. Use diaries or repeated studies for time, fatigue and context questions. Use surveys carefully to estimate frequency; convenience samples cannot establish population prevalence.
6. Combine findings with appropriately governed aggregate data. Keep participant-level information outside this repository.
7. Implement promising changes and assess outcomes with a suitable comparison design. Do not deliberately expose people to known serious access barriers to create a control group.
8. Publish limitations, contrary findings and what remains unknown.

## Outcome set

| Layer | Measures | Interpretation check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reach | Intended audience finding and accessing the service | Are excluded people missing from analytics? |
| Understanding | Comprehension of offer, choices and next steps | Does completion conceal misunderstanding? |
| Interaction | Completion, errors, recovery, effort and assistance | Is help chosen or forced? |
| Relationship | Repeat use, continuation, retention and successful exit | Are switching and life-stage effects considered? |
| Brand | Recognition, confidence, trust and relevance | Are measures comparable over time? |
| Business/service | Incremental contribution, resolution and cost changes | Are demand suppression and double-counting excluded? |

## Evaluation design

Prefer a design appropriate to the decision: moderated research for mechanisms; accessible surveys for reported experience; longitudinal cohorts for relationship changes; controlled or phased comparisons where appropriate for attribution. Record eligibility, recruitment, sample size rationale, uncertainty and missingness.

Do not choose a universal sample size or declare statistical significance without an analysis plan. Separate exploratory findings from confirmatory tests. Report practical effect and uncertainty, not only a p-value. Check for regressions in smaller groups; aggregate gains can conceal worse access.

## Evidence of effectiveness

Document the implementation actually tested, dates, comparison conditions, source revisions and remaining barriers. Customer validation is pending until real research occurs. Templates, synthetic examples and model-generated participant descriptions are not research findings.
