# Optimising a service for reach and engagement

## Start with the customer activity

Choose a complete activity, such as understanding a pension choice or booking and attending an appointment. Include discovery, eligibility, comparison, authentication, completion, confirmation, delivery, support and exit. A usable page cannot compensate for an inaccessible essential handoff.

## Practical workflow

1. **Frame the opportunity.** Record intended audience, brand purpose, service promise, current evidence and the decision to be made in the [organisation brief](../templates/organisation-brief.md).
2. **Estimate relevant scale.** Use age, geography and product context to select appropriate evidence. Keep potential audience, observed users and affected journeys separate.
3. **Map needs and circumstances.** Use the topic guides and include combinations relevant to the service. Capture preferences directly where possible.
4. **Find barriers.** Observe real tasks with customers, review research, assess content and service policies, and combine technical/manual evaluation. Record failure, excessive effort, lost confidence and dependence on unwanted help.
5. **Select improvements.** Retrieve the owning Double-FW guidance via the [resource map](../resources/double-fw-resource-map.md). Link every proposal to a barrier, task, customer outcome and evidence need.
6. **Integrate delivery systems.** Put recurring requirements into accessible UX design systems, accessible branding systems, content practice and service operations. Do not rely on a separate accessibility mode as the entire solution.
7. **Validate and measure.** Test the complete journey, including assistive technology, alternative modes and human support. Estimate opportunity before implementation and measure actual outcomes afterwards.
8. **Learn and maintain.** Assign ownership, track changes in audience and technology, and revise assumptions after evidence changes.

## Design and brand systems

For foundations such as typography, colour, labels, alternative text and content order, plan source guidance and meaningful manual and automatable checks. For components and patterns, plan design requirements, technical techniques, implementation examples, methodology and user task scripts. QA should trace those requirements to complete-journey outcomes.

Maintain brand recognition, trust, expectations, values, perceived value and resonance across access modes. A visually consistent experience that customers cannot understand does not deliver the brand promise. Preserve equivalent meaning across web, app, email, documents, phone and physical service.

## Agent-assisted services

Provide semantic content, understandable state, accessible controls and authorised interfaces so customers can use assistive tools or agents. Let customers choose and revise preferences. Treat a request for concise text as a preference; do not infer dyslexia. Confirm consequential actions through usable routes, preserve scope of authority and make correction and human handoff possible.

## Prioritisation

Assess customer consequence, reach evidence, relationship importance, likely benefit, delivery effort and dependencies. Small populations with severe barriers remain important. Avoid inflated ROI from adding overlapping demographics or assuming every affected customer is lost.

Output: the [opportunity assessment](../templates/opportunity-assessment.md), implementation requirements, source revisions and a measurement plan. Examples are in [the worked examples](../examples/retail-and-long-term-services.md).
