# UK, EU and US evidence: how to compare

## Established starting points

These are dated source observations checked on 14 November 2026, not harmonised estimates or claims that every source contains the latest possible dataset.

| Geography | Observation | Population and measure | Appropriate interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UK | 25%; 16.7 million, 2024–25 | FRS private-household population; source-defined disability | Population context; excludes some communal settings |
| EU | 23.9%, 2024 | People aged 16+; activity limitation, Eurostat news summary | EU context; not a functional-condition breakdown |
| US | 28.7%, 2022 BRFSS | Adults 18+; six functional disability domains | Functional-disability survey context; source methods matter |

Sources: [S04–S07](../evidence/source-register.md). The UK FRS also reports 12% of children, 23% of working-age adults and 45% of pension-age adults as disabled. Use the source's precise age definitions; pension age is not automatically age 65. These rates do not measure whether disability is apparent.

CDC explains that definitions and survey methods produce different estimates even with similar questions [S06]. Do not rank these countries or subtract the rates as if differences were real differences in prevalence under a common measure.

## Research procedure

For the UK, use FRS for source-defined disability and impairment groups, supplemented by Census and condition-specific evidence. For the EU, investigate EU-SILC activity-limitation data and relevant health surveys; retain member-state variation. For the US, compare ACS and BRFSS definitions, age coverage, survey mode and purpose before choosing a source.

For each source, record country coverage, private-household or institutional exclusions, age range, crude or standardised status, questionnaire wording, threshold, fieldwork dates and uncertainty. Retrieve the underlying tables and methodology before a new demographic calculation. The three headline observations above do not substitute for that work.

## Group breakdowns

Distinguish percentage of the whole population from percentage of disabled people. If 40% of a source-defined disabled subgroup reports mobility difficulty, that is not 40% of all adults. Multiplication is appropriate only when denominators, periods and populations match.

Keep functional domains, diagnosed conditions, equipment use, support use and language use in separate records. Mobility difficulty is not wheelchair use; hearing loss is not sign-language use; registration does not capture all sight loss. Categories commonly overlap and should not be rendered as a pie chart without a mutually exclusive classification.

## Next evidence releases

Prioritise compatible age-specific functional tables, neurodevelopmental prevalence studies and longitudinal evidence. Check source revisions before every publication. Record gaps explicitly rather than filling EU or US cells with UK ratios. The [research backlog](../RESEARCH_BACKLOG.md) owns the remaining work.

## Expanded statistics and derivations

Use [sections 2–4 of the statistical reference](../Accessibility_Demographic_Statistics_and_Estimation_Methods.md#2-uk-disability-and-functional-needs) for UK01–UK07, DR01–DR06, EU01–EU05 and US01–US09. The derived DR records are disability-and-impairment intersections, not all people with a functional difficulty. EU digital-participation measures concern ages 16–74 and their stated recall periods; they do not share the age base of EU01.

Use [F03](../Accessibility_Demographic_Statistics_and_Estimation_Methods.md#f03--conditional-proportions-and-denominator-correction) to convert a proportion among disabled respondents into a compatible whole-group joint estimate. Keep thresholds and crude/standardised status visible. Source IDs S04–S07 remain valid aliases through [the source register](../evidence/source-register.md), rather than separate supporting studies.
