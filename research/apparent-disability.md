# Investigating apparent disability

## Question

What proportion of people have a disability that may be noticeable during a specified interaction, and what does that tell us about customer opportunity?

Apparency is context-dependent. An impairment may be apparent in a physical interaction and not in a text-based service. Equipment, communication, activity, observer knowledge and disclosure affect what is noticed. A diagnosis alone does not establish apparency.

## Supplied starting point

The [supplied UK report](../evidence/supplied/Apparent_Disability_Proportions_UK_Report.md) was written for casting and portrayal. It proposes a 6–8% working-age range and 7% midpoint. This repository does not endorse those as validated prevalence estimates: see [the review](../evidence/apparent-disability-review.md).

## Method

1. Define the context: ordinary in-person customer interaction, video encounter or portrayal study, for example. Do not silently move between them.
2. Define the target population and operational criterion, with disabled contributors. Include uncertainty and a not-determinable category.
3. Separate condition prevalence, equipment use, self-described apparency and observer recognition. Research can examine these relationships but should not treat them as equivalent.
4. Identify suitable self-report or observational evidence with clear consent. Do not build image-based diagnosis or surveillance tools.
5. Use compatible age-specific denominators, account for overlap, and publish the model and sensitivity analysis if estimating a combined proportion.
6. Translate to service needs only after examining relevant tasks. Being apparent does not determine the amount or type of support required.

For the EU and US, investigate independently. Do not export the supplied UK estimate. If no direct measure exists, publish that finding and an evidence-development plan instead of an unsupported national total.

## Why keep this topic?

It can inform representation, assumptions about who is noticed, and the limitations of staff recognition. It must not become the gatekeeper for accessible service: people with non-apparent needs need usable routes and optional support too.

Output: context-specific definition, evidence table, explicit unknowns, overlap model if justified, and implications for representation and service design. Use [definitions](../foundations/disability-models-and-access-needs.md) and [estimation](../methods/estimating-demographics.md).
