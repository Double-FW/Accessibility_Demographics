# Ageing and changing customer needs

## Research question

How do changing functional needs and population age structures affect reach, engagement and sustained customer relationships? Age is a segmentation variable, not a diagnosis or proxy for digital incompetence.

W3C's older-user work connects many age-related web needs with accessibility guidance [S08]. This supports joint design work, not a claim that conformance alone assures usability.

## Evidence plan

| Dimension | Investigate | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hearing | Functional hearing, speech-in-noise demands, preferred communication | Equating all hearing loss with sign-language use |
| Vision | Corrected and uncorrected functional vision, contrast, glare, reading | Treating all glasses wearers as disabled or all sight loss as blindness |
| Motor | Dexterity, reach, steadiness, mobility and fatigue in the task | Assuming mouse, touch and speech are interchangeable |
| Cognitive | Memory, attention, comprehension, learning and decision demands | Conflating normal variation, diagnosis and incapacity |

For each geography, retrieve compatible age-specific functional estimates and official population projections. Projected audience = sum(projected population in age band × chosen age-specific prevalence). This assumes rates remain applicable; show alternatives where evidence supports change. Do not confuse the proportion of older people with the proportion experiencing a particular impairment.

## Service relationships

For long-term products, examine acquisition, periodic engagement, significant decisions, authentication, changes of circumstances, support and exit. Customers may acquire a new need, change preference, use assistive technology or rely on a chosen supporter. Maintain agency and privacy; assistance does not automatically imply inability to decide.

## Research and design

Recruit by functional need, experience and context as well as age. Include older people with varied digital familiarity and younger people with similar needs. Investigate readable information, time flexibility, recoverable errors, accessible authentication, task continuity and support that retains context.

Measure independent and chosen-assisted completion separately, comprehension, effort, confidence, continued use and retention. Avoid treating compulsory help as success equivalent to independent access.
