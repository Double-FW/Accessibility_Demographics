# Illness, injury, distress and demanding services

## Scope

Include temporary illness, recovery, injury, pain, fatigue, fluctuating conditions, grief, emotional distress and stressful service circumstances. Include bereavement, healthcare, justice, emergency support, financial difficulty, housing and other services where the situation can make access especially demanding.

Do not classify all people using these services as disabled or unable to decide. The aim is a service that remains understandable, usable and supportive when circumstances are difficult.

## Assess service relevance

Ask whether the service routinely attracts people experiencing difficulty; creates an emotionally demanding task; requires unfamiliar decisions; sets deadlines; asks for repeated evidence; limits channels; or makes errors hard to reverse. Assess both prevalence within this service and the consequence of a failed interaction.

| Service | Circumstance to investigate | Opportunity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bereavement | Repeating a death notification across departments | Coordinated notification, progress visibility and optional human support |
| Healthcare | Unfamiliar information, symptoms, fatigue or anxiety | Clear preparation, accessible booking, alternative communication and continuity |
| Justice | Stress, unfamiliar terminology and consequential deadlines | Understandable steps, accessible documents, reminders and assistance routes |
| Financial difficulty | High stakes, shame or interrupted attention | Clear choices, non-judgemental language, pause/resume and contextual support |
| Injury or recovery | Temporary difficulty using a usual input method | Flexible channels and controls without permanent reclassification |

## Research approach

Work with people with lived experience and appropriate service specialists. Offer accessible participation, breaks, withdrawal and choice of communication. Avoid unnecessary requests to retell traumatic experiences. Research ordinary task demands and support options without manufacturing distress.

Use aggregate service evidence, accessible interviews, journey walkthroughs and carefully designed diaries where suitable. Review who fails to reach the research or service channel. Staff observations are useful but cannot replace customers' accounts or establish population prevalence.

## Operational implications

Explore time flexibility, plain explanations, save-and-return, trusted or authorised assistance, accessible identity checks, progress visibility, clear confirmation and correction. Support users who want independence and those choosing help. Preserve security and privacy through accessible routes; do not bypass them by assuming distress authorises disclosure.

## Measures

Record successful access to the intended service, comprehension, time and effort, avoidable repetition, unwanted assistance, confidence, escalation quality and resolution. Evaluate service benefit alongside cost; fewer support contacts can mean a better journey or blocked support, so interpretation needs evidence.

Use the [service optimisation guide](../guides/service-optimisation.md) and [research plan](../guides/research-and-measurement.md).

## Evidence and service-specific estimates

Use MH01–MH05 and TR01–TR09 in [section 7 of the statistical reference](../Accessibility_Demographic_Statistics_and_Estimation_Methods.md#7-sleep-mental-health-illness-injury-and-transient-circumstances) as bounded context. HSE records concern work-related conditions and absence; they do not measure all illness, all injury episodes or every day of functional difficulty. Mental-health prevalence does not establish decision-making incapacity, grief duration or an impairment rate for every support service.

Apply [F11](../Accessibility_Demographic_Statistics_and_Estimation_Methods.md#f11--service-specific-selection) to the actual service audience, supported by local research. Use [F06](../Accessibility_Demographic_Statistics_and_Estimation_Methods.md#f06--episode-burden-and-average-time-affected)–[F09](../Accessibility_Demographic_Statistics_and_Estimation_Methods.md#f09--observed-person-time-and-interaction-exposure) to distinguish episode flows, average occupied time, unique people and affected journeys. Bereavement, justice, healthcare and support tasks still need service-selected evidence. No universal multiplier from deaths to grieving customers is adopted.
