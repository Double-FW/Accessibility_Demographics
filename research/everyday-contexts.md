# Everyday contexts and changing interaction

## Purpose

Investigate how ordinary circumstances affect access and where flexible service design expands usable reach. The scenarios below are research hypotheses, not prevalence findings.

| Context | Potential task constraint | Opportunity to investigate | Evidence to collect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dimmed display or bright glare | Content and subtle cues hard to distinguish | Resilient contrast, readable type, non-colour cues | Perception and completion in realistic displays |
| Sound muted or noisy setting | Audio information unavailable | Captions, text equivalents, visual feedback | Understanding with audio unavailable |
| One hand occupied, left or right | Limited reach and precision | Reachable actions, alternatives to complex gestures | Both-hand preference and one-handed tasks |
| Crowded transport or interruptions | Unstable input and lost task context | Save state, resumable flows, clear progress | Recovery and repeated effort |
| Beginning/end of day; shift work | Task demands may interact with sleep and fatigue | Flexible timing and lower memory burden | Repeated observations, sleep/shift context and task effects |
| Speech preferred for efficiency | User wants another input/output mode | User-controlled speech alongside usable alternatives | Privacy, correction, noise and actual preference |
| Alcohol, medication effects or recreational substances | Some circumstances may affect perception or control | Clear feedback, recovery and support | Suitable existing research; avoid assumptions about individuals |
| Driving or other safety-critical activity | Attention must remain on the primary activity | Defer demanding tasks, safe stopping/resumption | Task suitability; voice is not proof of safety |

Do not claim everyone has lower capacity at the same clock times. Investigate individual patterns, sleep, shift work and task demands. Medication may improve functioning, have side effects or have no relevant effect; the presence of a prescription is not an impairment indicator.

## Exposure research

Use accessible diaries, interviews, voluntary context questions, journey observations and appropriately governed aggregate analytics. Ask when and why a mode was chosen. Muted audio, low brightness or one-handed use cannot establish a disability or a person's reason for using that setting.

Estimate context frequency in the target audience and period; do not borrow a general smartphone statistic as the frequency of a particular product task. For combinations, use joint evidence or explicit assumptions under [combined reach](../methods/combined-needs-and-reach.md).

## Scenario record

Record person/need where volunteered; state; environment/device; task; relationship; barrier; preferred option; observed outcome; exposure evidence; improvement; evidence status. Test whether an alternative helps or introduces a new conflict, such as speech output revealing private information in public.

Never ask participants to become intoxicated, drive while testing a distracting interface or reproduce unsafe travel conditions. Investigate safely through appropriate existing evidence and controlled alternatives.

W3C discusses situational limitations such as bright sunlight and inability to listen to audio [S03]. The specific service impacts above require local research, not extrapolation from that general observation.
