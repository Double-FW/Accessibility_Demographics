# Review of the supplied apparent-disability estimate

## Status

Editorial and methodological review of the supplied report; not a completed external-source audit. Preserve the original unchanged. No new population estimate is established here.

## What is useful

The report recognises context-dependent apparency, mixed denominators, incomplete service registers and overlapping groups. It distinguishes sign-language main-language counts from all sign-language users and rejects a combined miscellaneous category.

## Why its headline is not adopted

| Issue | Consequence | Work needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mobility impairment is narrowed to apparent mobility without a reported measurement model | Plausibility is substituted for an observed proportion | Direct evidence or explicit sensitivity assumptions |
| Working-age wheelchair and sensory estimates use approximate age adjustments | Population and threshold may not match | Age-specific evidence and reproducible calculations |
| Severe hearing loss and registration are used as apparency proxies | Functional severity does not establish observer recognition | Separate functional prevalence from context-specific apparency |
| BSL main-language figure covers England and Wales, age 3+, not the target UK working-age population | Denominator and geography mismatch | Target-population estimate or keep as contextual evidence only |
| Total says overlap is allowed for but provides no joint estimates or calculation | The 6–8% range cannot be reproduced | Publish the union model and sensitivity analysis |
| Range and midpoint are called credible without a documented uncertainty model | Planning assumption can be mistaken for established evidence | Label as unvalidated hypothesis; withhold benchmark use |

The listed component ranges do not independently validate the final range. Unmeasured other categories and unknown intersections prevent a reliable total. A midpoint is arithmetic, not additional evidence.

## Reuse rule

The supplied file remains background with attribution to user-supplied material, date received 14 November 2026, authorship not independently verified. Do not present 6–8% or 7% as an accepted benchmark, official statistic or transferable EU/US estimate. If discussing them, state their provenance and unvalidated status beside the numbers.
