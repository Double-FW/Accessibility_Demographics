# Objectives and scope

## Purpose

Help organisations discover the opportunity to reach, engage and retain customers through inclusive and universal design. Start with what customers need to achieve, then ask how products, communications, environments and service policies enable it.

## Objectives and evidence of progress

| Objective | Useful output | Evidence of progress |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Understand relevant audiences | Attributed demographic ranges | Estimates match geography, age and definition |
| Understand changing access | Task and circumstance scenarios | Customers confirm barriers and coping strategies |
| Improve reach | Discoverable and usable routes into services | More intended customers can find and understand the offer |
| Improve engagement | Comprehensible, controllable journeys | Less avoidable effort; greater confidence and successful participation |
| Sustain relationships | Support for changing needs and channel choice | Better continued use, retention and recovery after difficulty |
| Demonstrate value | Separate financial, brand and customer outcomes | Measured changes with attribution limits |

## Scope

UK, EU and US population evidence; apparent and non-apparent disabilities; neurodivergence; sensory and perceptual differences; ageing; fluctuating conditions; illness and injury; emotional distress; environmental and situational constraints; preferences and assistive strategies. Include digital, physical, communication and human-support touchpoints across complete customer journeys.

Use the five dimensions: person, current state, context, task and relationship. None is a substitute for another. Disability status, functional need and design preference are related but distinct.

## Boundaries

This is opportunity research, not a regulatory-risk framework, diagnosis tool, individual health-profiling system, clinical advice service or universal market-size claim. Standards and specialist QA may support implementation, but conformance results do not quantify market opportunity or prove satisfaction.

Universal design is an ambition to support the broadest practical participation. It does not establish that one mode or identical treatment suits everyone. Alternatives and personal control can be necessary parts of a shared service.

## Decision cycle

Define a journey and audience; gather evidence; estimate scale with uncertainty; identify barriers with customers; select improvements; assess benefits and trade-offs; implement and evaluate; revise the estimate and decision.

Record the decision owner, research owner, design owner and measurement owner. Prioritise using customer consequence, evidence strength, potential reach, relationship value, feasibility and dependencies. Avoid a single score that hides exclusions or unsupported assumptions.
